How to probe
  1. Power off and unplug. Take the card out if that makes the fingers easier to reach.
  2. Lift the ZIF lever and remove the CPU. You probe the empty socket holes, so the meter touches the same metal the CPU pins would.
  3. Find which way the socket runs. Touch one probe to a known ground (a mounting screw or the power connector's black wire) and step along each outer edge of the socket. The edge where holes 8 to 12 all beep, with more at 6 and 14, is row S. Count from the end where the 6th hole is the first to beep: that is S1. Row A is the opposite edge. Check the result on the map.
  4. Set the meter to continuity (beep). Put one probe on finger 1 and sweep the other along the socket holes until it beeps. Type that hole into the log (for example Q14).
  5. If nothing in the socket beeps, test the finger against ground and against a 5 V hole (B7). If it's neither, it goes to another chip: follow the trace and note where it ends, for example U41 pin 52.
  6. Number the fingers from the left end (the barcode label side) with the chips facing you: that is side A. Side B is the back, numbered from the same end.

What a cache needs

Green means the signal has turned up on some finger. Fingers that go to other chips are usually the cache controls: SRAM chip select, output enable, write enables and the tag lines.
